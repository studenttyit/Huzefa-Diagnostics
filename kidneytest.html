Kidney Tests

Kidneys are undoubtedly one of the most important organs of our body, performing blood filtration. The kidneys filter waste from the blood and produce urine. Without proper kidney functioning, our body cannot survive. Hence, attention should be paid to ensure they are in good health at all times. Any signs of dysfunction or disease should be checked with a doctor immediately.

Kidney tests are usually performed in clinics or hospitals, but certain preliminary tests can be conducted at home before you consult a doctor. If you want to easily get kidney tests done at home, a simple blood test or urine test can be conducted from home.

When is a Kidney Test Prescribed?

A kidney test is generally prescribed when there’s reason to believe that there could be a problem with the functioning of the kidneys. This could be due to a variety of factors, including a family history of kidney problems, current or previous medical conditions, or medications that can affect kidney function.?

A kidney test can also be prescribed if you have symptoms of any kidney-related issues such as

  • Pain in the kidney
  • Urinary tract infections
  • Blood in urine?
  • Hypertension (high blood pressure)
  • Appetite loss
  • Illness
  • Kidney stones

These symptoms can also include a change in urination habits, change in urine color, pain in the abdomen or back, or swelling in extreme situations.

If you are experiencing any of the abovementioned symptoms, you should consult a health expert or a kidney doctor immediately. Such symptoms can be indicative of a problem with kidneys, which could be a disease, disorder, or a short term-ailment.

Why Are Kidney Tests Important?

Kidney tests can help you identify kidney diseases or disorders in the early stages. Getting the treatment soon can help you eliminate kidney diseases effectively as well as manage the symptoms, enhancing the quality of your daily life.

Routine kidney tests are often prescribed by doctors to keep kidney functions in check. If your kidney function is impaired, it can lead to other serious health problems like heart disease, stroke, and kidney failure.

A kidney test can help your doctor to check for kidney damage from an infection or injury. Additionally, the kidneys regulate the synthesis of red blood cells, electrolyte balance, and blood pressure, which are all vital functions of the body. This is why it is important to detect any problems early on.

How is a Kidney Test Performed?

Multiple types of kidney tests are performed to check the complete health status and functioning of a person's kidneys, as well as to detect any abnormalities. They are as follows:

  • Urine Tests

    • The likes of Urinalysis, Urine Protein, Microalbuminuria, Albumin to Creatinine Ratio, and Creatinine Clearance help to identify abnormalities in the kidney functions caused during the filtering of waste material.

  • Blood Tests

    • Blood Tests such as Serum Creatinine, GFR, and BUN help to identify kidney diseases or dysfunctions through blood sampling.

  • Imaging Tests

    • Ultrasounds and CT Scans are often prescribed during kidney checkups to look for abnormalities such as kidney stones, tumors, ruptures, or injuries in the kidneys.

  • Kidney Biopsy

    • A biopsy can also be done to identify the extent of damage that has occurred to the kidney or in more serious cases when the patient needs a transplant.

Simple tests like that of urine and blood for checking kidney health can be performed at home, by collecting samples and sending them to the laboratory for testing. These are also looked upon as preliminary tests before prescribing other seemingly more expensive tests. However, Kidney tests should be performed only under trained medical practitioners to ensure correct procedure and accurate test results.
